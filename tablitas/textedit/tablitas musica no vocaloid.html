This song is named “La vita nuova” after Dante Alighieri’s first known work “Vita Nuova”, which literally translates to “New Life”.

The Christine and the Queens' song is a transposition of the medieval conception of courtly love that is developed in this book. Rewritten in a contemporary scene, the idea of love has not changed that much in almost a millennium: two people who want to be touched, two people who are waiting to be touched.

kyari pamyu pamyu

2011

I think it has to do a lot with Japanese attitudes toward the ephemerality of life. Kyari Pamyu Pamyu is dressed in lolita style that seems to make a statement of youth - the bloomers, the scrunched up socks, the bows in her hair all point to that sort of childlike nature.


Essentially, the video contrasts images of death - black birds, tanks, hockey masks, bones - with the cheery, youthful attitude of the lyrics. The song is basically saying that life goes by too fast to sweat the small stuff, so get out there and have fun, even if it's kind of childish. It also plays into the notion that life is beautiful precisely because it goes by so fast.


What the hell half of the other stuff in the video means - the fat lady with the blacked out face, the pieces of bread, the eyeballs, all that stuff? You got me. I think it's just meant to be weird and quirky and insane like that. Or maybe it's another layer of meaning that I can't quite understand. You've always got to consider the "fallacy of intent" here - some things just don't necessarily mean any one thing. That's the fun of literary analysis. link
